3. How to study each lecture: the 5-step method

  1. Skim (5 min). Read the lecture's topic list in this guide, then flip through the slide once.
  2. Redraw (15 to 20 min). Close the slide. Draw every circuit and waveform yourself. Waveforms must be drawn "with proper alignment" (stacked on the same ωt axis). Many marks depend on this.
  3. Derive (15 to 20 min). Write each derivation from start to boxed result without looking. If you get stuck, look once, then start again from the top.
  4. Solve (20 to 30 min). Solve every "Do with your own hand" item with the book closed. Compare every number with this guide.
  5. Recall (5 min). Answer the self-test questions out loud or on paper. Tick the boxes only when you can do it without help. Copy formulas onto your one-page formula sheet.
Rules for 4 days: Do not read passively. Writing beats reading. Study in 50-minute blocks with 10-minute breaks. Use the checklist file (Topic_Checklist.html) to tick off every topic. Sleep at least 6 hours each night; memory consolidates during sleep.

Part A · ★★★ Must master · 1.25 h · Day 1 · Rashid ch.9
Slide: Lecture 07 SCR.pdf

Topics in this lecture (11)

7. In the exam hall

  1. Read the whole paper first (3 minutes). Choose the optional question in each part by asking which one has more numericals you have practised.
  2. Time: about 2 minutes per mark. A 10-mark question gets about 20 minutes. Keep 10 minutes at the end to review.
  3. Start each part with your strongest compulsory question.
  4. Derivations: circuit, then equivalent circuits, then aligned waveforms, then equations, then boxed result.
  5. Numericals: formula, substitution, answer with unit, boxed. Partial marks depend on the formula line.
  6. Compare / differentiate: always a table of at least 5 rows.
  7. Surprise question: write the governing equation and draw the circuit anyway. Never leave a sub-part blank.
